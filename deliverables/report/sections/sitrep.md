# Summary numbers and the one-page situation report

**What it does.** The last step of every run (`python -m povodne.report <run>`) turns the outputs of all earlier steps into one file,
`stats.json`, which is the **single source of every number** shown to users. The dashboard and the one-page situation report
(`report/sitrep_en.pdf` + `.md`) only display numbers from this file; no text is generated by a language model and nothing is
estimated outside the pipeline. The same step also draws the map images the dashboard shows (flood probability, optical images
before/after, radar shadow and "could not be seen" areas), reprojected to longitude/latitude so they align with the base map.

**It always runs.** If downloading, preprocessing or the flood model fails, the report still runs and says so: an empty map must
never look like "no flood". Missing parts of a run are written as *missing* (`null`), not as zero; zero means "computed, nothing found".

**Verdict.** Each run ends with one of three answers, with the reasons in plain English:
- *Insufficient data* — no flood map was produced, or radar and clear optical images together observe less than **20 %** of the area.
- *Flood detected* — at least **0.1 km²** of new water or debris/mud in the observed part of the area.
- *No significant flooding* — otherwise, always stated together with how much of the area was actually observed.
Both thresholds are configurable practical choices (0.1 km² ≈ ten of the smallest patches the flood step keeps); they were not tuned
on the Copernicus EMS case-study maps.

**What the sitrep contains (one A4 page).** The verdict; the date and time of the latest satellite image after the event and how
many days after the event it was taken (rescuers must know how old the picture is); key numbers (new water, debris by type, buildings
hit as a range "certain – including possible", blocked roads, blocked/suspect bridges, settlements cut off and possibly cut off,
important places hit or cut off, unverified helicopter landing candidates); a small map; the most urgent settlements ranked by impact
level and size with the reason for each; which road repairs reconnect the most buildings first; data quality (observed share, radar
visibility, cloud cover, estimated false share of debris from the flood-free self-test) and the main warnings; the required data
attributions and the note that this is an educational prototype, preliminary and not validated in the field. When the content does
not fit, tables are shortened until the PDF has exactly one page.

**Limitations.** The report can only be as good as the steps before it: numbers of buildings and roads depend on OpenStreetMap
completeness before the event, debris areas are indicative, and a settlement without an OSM name is listed by its type. Population is
shown only where OpenStreetMap has it — we do not estimate the number of people affected. Names in scripts the PDF font cannot draw
(e.g. Devanagari) are replaced by the place type and OSM id in the PDF (the Markdown version keeps them). The sitrep is in English only.
