## Zadání Track B (přepis originálu, anglicky)

### Challenge
When roads, bridges and phone lines are gone, rescuers still need to know where the damage is and who is cut off.
Build the system that tells them — from space.

### Context
- On 26 August 2026, the partial collapse of a high-altitude glacier in northern Nepal triggered an avalanche of ice and
  rock that sent a flood of water, mud and debris down the **Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river corridor**. Nepal's National
  Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority reported 1,342 deaths by 6 September, with almost 4,900 people still
  missing or out of contact (NDRRMA situation report, as reported by the Copernicus Emergency Management Service).
- In disasters like this, ground sensors can be swept away, and roads, bridges and communications fail. Rescuers urgently
  need to know where the damage is worst and which villages have been cut off.
- Free satellite data covers every valley of the Himalaya. Radar satellites see through monsoon clouds, and optical
  satellites show damage in detail. The challenge is turning that raw data into trustworthy answers, quickly enough to matter.

### Requirements
Build a system that takes **an area and a flood date** and uses satellite images to answer three questions for rescue teams:
1. **Where did the flood hit?** Map flooded and debris-covered areas by comparing images from before and after the event.
   Use Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through monsoon clouds, and Sentinel-2 optical images when the sky is clear.
2. **What was damaged?** Overlay buildings, roads and bridges from OpenStreetMap and estimate which ones were hit.
3. **Who is cut off?** Using the road network, find settlements that no longer have a road connection to the nearest
   town or hospital.

**AI component – choose one of the following:**
- **Flood segmentation model** ← NÁŠ VÝBĚR — Train a model to detect water or debris in satellite images using the
  listed training data, and show how well it works on Himalayan scenes it has never seen.
- Situation-report copilot — An assistant that answers rescuers' questions and writes short situation reports in English
  and Nepali. Every number must come from your system's maps, never invented by the language model.

**Bonus:** Given any point upstream, trace the flood path down the valley using elevation data and list the settlements along it.

**Case study:** Map the August 2026 Trishuli flood and compare your results with the official Copernicus Emergency
Management Service maps for this event (**activation EMSR927**).

Ilustrace z brožury ("WHO IS CUT OFF?"): povodeň (zlatě) zničila dva úseky silnice (červeně); vesnice B a C se už
nedostanou po silnici do okresní nemocnice, vesnice A a D ano. *"This is the kind of answer your system should produce
automatically, for any valley the judges choose."*

### Technical considerations — data rules
- **Allowed as inputs:** Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Copernicus DEM, OpenStreetMap as it was before the event, and the listed
  training datasets.
- **Not allowed as inputs:** Copernicus EMS, UNOSAT or any other published damage maps, and OpenStreetMap edits made after
  the event. These may only be used to check your results. **Breaking this rule leads to disqualification.**
- The system must **run end-to-end from raw satellite data for any area and date the judges choose**.
- **Tip:** Sentinel-1 radar images of the corridor exist from a few days before and after the event. For change detection,
  compare images from the **same orbit track (same time of day, 12 days apart)**. Images from different tracks view the
  terrain from different angles and cannot be compared pixel by pixel.

## Naše interpretace / co z toho plyne pro práci
- **Robustnost na libovolnou AOI a datum je klíčová** (live hodnocení). Pipeline musí sama najít scény (S1 stejný track
  pre/post, S2 s nízkou oblačností), stáhnout DEM a OSM, a doběhnout v rozumném čase (cíl: < ~15 min pro údolí ~30×30 km).
  Musí mít rozumné chování, když S2 je zatažené (jen S1) nebo když pro dané datum chybí scény.
- Himálaj = strmý terén → SAR layover/shadow, radiometrický terén. Potřebujeme terrain-corrected (ideálně RTC) backscatter
  a masku layover/shadow + svah/HAND z DEM, jinak budou falešné pozitivy.
- Debris flow ≠ jen voda: bahno/kamení může mít v SAR **vyšší** backscatter (drsnost) a v S2 vypadá jako holá půda.
  Rozlišujeme třídy *flood water* a *debris* (viz ARCHITECTURE).
- AI komponent (25 %): musíme ukázat **metriky na himálajských scénách, které model neviděl** (held-out test) — ne jen
  validaci na Kuro Siwo. Porovnání s baseline (prahování/change detection) je silný argument.
- Cut-off analýza (20 %): settlement → nejbližší town/hospital přes silniční graf před a po (odstranění zasažených úseků a mostů).
- Honesty about limitations (součást 15 %): průběžně si zapisovat omezení do `docs/DECISIONS.md` / PROGRESS, ať je report snadný.
